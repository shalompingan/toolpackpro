How Many Coats of Paint Do I Need?

Short answer: two coats, for almost every project. That's the standard for a smooth, even, long-lasting finish — and it's what most professional painters use by default, even when a single coat looks "good enough" right after drying.

But the right number can range from one coat to four, depending on what you're covering and what color you're using. Here's how to tell which situation you're in.

The Short Answer: Two Coats (for Most Projects)

If you're repainting a wall in a new color, on a normal (non-textured) surface, with a standard flat or eggshell paint — plan on two coats. The first coat rarely covers evenly on its own; it can look patchy or let the old color show through in spots. The second coat evens everything out and gives you the durable finish that actually lasts.

This is also the assumption most paint calculators use by default, including ours — so if you're not sure, two coats is the safe number to plan around.

When You Might Need 3–4 Coats

A few situations push the number higher:

  • Big color change. Going from a dark wall to a light color (or light to dark) usually needs an extra coat or two to fully cover the old color. Red, orange, and other saturated colors are notorious for needing 3+ coats even over similar shades.
  • New or bare drywall. Unpainted drywall is porous and soaks up paint unevenly. The standard approach is one coat of primer, then two coats of finish paint — three coats total.
  • Textured walls. Texture (knockdown, orange peel, stucco) creates more surface area than a flat wall, so it eats more paint and often needs an extra coat to look fully even.
  • Semi-gloss or glossy paint. Higher-sheen paints show inconsistencies more than flat paint does, so jobs that need 2 coats in flat finish often need 3 in semi-gloss.

When One Coat Might Be Enough

If you're repainting a wall the same color, and the existing paint is in good condition (no stains, no patches, no fading), one coat can be enough — especially with a high-quality paint-and-primer-in-one product. That said, most pros still recommend a second coat if the old paint has faded at all, which is common even when a wall "looks fine."

How Coats Affect How Much Paint You Need

The number of coats has a direct, multiplying effect on how much paint to buy — going from 1 coat to 2 roughly doubles your gallons needed, and 3 coats roughly triples it. This is the single biggest factor people get wrong when estimating paint for a project, more than room size or paint brand.
